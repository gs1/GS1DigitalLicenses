Abstract

This document describes the GS1 Digital License Ecosystem. GS1 and its country-based Member Organizations (MO plural MOs) form a federated system that issues unique identifiers to its Member Companies (MC plural MCs) for trade items and entities including -- but not limited to -- GLNs (singular GLN), GTINs (singular GTIN), GRAIs (singular GRAI), SSCCs (singular SSCC) , and other GS1 ID Keys. As trade communications becomes increasingly digital, GS1 is extending it's license system to provide digital licenses using W3C Verifiable Credentials (VCs singular VC). These GS1 License Credentials allow companies to prove their authority over their GS1 ID Keys and product data. This document is intended for MO and MC IT architects and developers who wish to understand and use GS1 License Credentials to issue trusted GS1 ID keys and verifiable product data.

Overview

This section presents an overview of GS1 and key concepts that are required to understand and use GS1 License Credentials

About GS1

GS1 is a neutral, global collaboration platform that brings industry leaders, government, regulators, academia, and associations together to develop standards-based solutions to address the challenges of data exchange. Our scale and reach—local Member Organisations (MOs) in 118 countries, over two million member companies (MCs) and 10 billion transactions every day—helps ensure that there is a common language of business across the globe.

The sound of a beep at the checkout counter. The GS1 barcode launched a digital revolution that forever changed the way the world does business. With a simple scan a product could be identified and connected to a computerised system. Companies of all sizes need barcode numbers to correctly identify and sell their products in stores or online. The power of the data captured through the barcode is helping to solve large and complex industry challenges.

Consumers, patients, business partners and regulators are demanding more and better product data than ever before—but complex and costly data exchange processes are producing a surge in low-quality data. GS1 empowers industry’s digital transformation by enabling businesses to create a digital version of a product that is as good as the physical one. Ultimately, consumers and patients will benefit from increased product transparency and the seamless experiences that this brings.

GS1 standards are the most widely used system of standards in the world. By enabling the sharing of trusted quality data everywhere, they tell the story behind products—where they originated, what they contain and where they have been during their supply-chain journey. This, in turn, removes friction between business partners and improves performance and safety across the entire supply chain.

GS1 Idenitfication Keys (GS1 ID Keys)

GS1 ID Keys give companies efficient ways to access information about items in their supply chains, and share this information with trading partners. GS1 ID Keys enable organisations to assign standard identifiers to products, documents, physical locations and more. Because GS1 ID Keys are globally unique, they can be shared between organisations, increasing supply chain visibility for trading partners. To build a GS1 ID Key, your organisation must become a member of GS1 and obtain a GS1 Company Prefix (GCP). This will form the basis of your GS1 ID Keys. There are twelve Tier 1 GS1 ID Keys, allowing organisations to connect physical events and related information, as in the table below.

The GS1 System supports various Keys not directly administered by GS1. These are referred to as Tier 2 - 4 Keys. They are addressed in 10. Tricky Topics.

GS1 Licenses

MCs need to obtain and maintain a license to create GS1 ID Keys. Companies can license GCPs or individual GS1 ID Keys from any GS1 MO regardless of their physical location. License types and terms vary. See Section 3. GS1 License Credentials for details. For more on licensing from GS1, see https://www.gs1.org/standards/id-keys/company-prefix".

Three license types are relevant to the current Digital License System:

  • GS1 Prefix Licenses
  • GS1 Company Prefix Licenses (GCPs singular GCP)
  • GS1 Single Issue Key Licenses

GS1 Prefix Licenses

GS1 Prefix Licenses are issued by the GS1 Global Office (GO) to GS1 MOs throughout the world. These prefix licenses are short numbers that authorize the MO to issue GS1 Company Prefixes and GS1 ID Keys to its members. For example the prefixes 500 - 509 are licensed to GS1 UK.

GS1 Company Prefix Licenses

GCPs are typically issued by an MO to a Member Company. MOs are only allowed to issue GCPs that begin with one of the GS1 Prefix License issued to them by GS1 GO. For example, the GCP 5000358 was issued to Tesco Stores Limited by GS1 UK; a GCP starts with the GS1 Prefix 500 which is licensed to GS1 UK by GS1 GO.

A company with a valid GCP can issue any GS1 ID Key that begins with this prefix according to the GS1 assignment guidelines (quick reference card).

GS1 Single Issue Licenses

Some MOs issue licenses for individual GS1 ID Keys, typically GTIN or GLN. This is common practice for small companies needing only a few GTINs or companies that need a GLN but are not creating their own products or shipments. In addition, for size-constrained producs MCs can license individual GTINs called GTIN-8 which have a more compact barcode representation.

GS1 Digital Links

GS1 Digital Link is the standard method for encoding GS1 ID Keys and select product attributes into GS1 Application Identifiers (AI) formatted as a (URI). These GS1 Identifiers can then be:

  • Encoded in a QR code or DataMatrix and scanned.
  • Connected to online information.
  • Used in data standards where URIs are required.

The standard can be used in different ways depending on the business requirements arising from different use cases.

For example, in healthcare, GS1 identifiers are usually encoded in a GS1 DataMatrix and converted into a GS1 Digital Link URI within the scanning software, usually a mobile phone app. In other cases the app connects to a resolver which returns the link types associated with the specific GTIN. The Digital Link URI is not encoded in a 2D barcode in healthcare applications.

In retail, GS1 Digital Link is an important part of industry’s global migration to 2D barcodes where the vision is that each product will have a single multipurpose 2D barcode that can be used at the retail point-of-sale, and to access digital content such as certifications, instructions for use, product information, traceability information and more.

Use of the GS1 Digital Link standard means that identifiers such as the GTIN are now a gateway to information that strengthens brand loyalty, improves supply chain traceability information, and enables business partner APIs, patient safety information and more.

Using the GS1 standard means you’re not only providing a URL for people to scan, you’re also providing GS1 identifiers – the same identifiers relied upon throughout industry today – and leveraging open global standards. The brand owner can link from the GS1 Digital Link URI to any number of digital information sources, all from one barcode symbol, saving space and improving efficiency. Digital content can be continually updated by a brand owner, without the need to change the QR Code with GS1 Digital Link URI on-pack, meaning packaging or artwork does not need to be changed for access to things like new promotions or usage ideas. Meanwhile, retailers, supply chain partners, and third-party applications, like diet and health apps, can still make use of the barcode as an entry point to look up their own information in their IT systems, exactly as they do today.

GS1 Digital Link can represent all GS1 ID Keys and many relevant AIs including GTIN, Batch/Lot, Expiration Date, Serial Number etc., allowing a single URI format to represent See [gs1-digital-link] for more details.

GS1 License Credentials

GS1 Licensing provides the root of trust for the globally unique trade identification systems. GS1 License Credentials are digital representations of the existing GS1 Licenses described in 2.3 GS1 Licenses. These licenses can be verified by machines in B2B transactions. Why might digital verification of GS1 Licenses be beneficial?

  • To prove a GS1 MO is authentic within the GS1 system for issuing licenses
  • To prove a MC or MO is authorized to create specific GS1 ID Keys
  • To establish provenance over GS1 ID Keys created by MC or MO
  • To establish provenance over data about GS1 ID Keys created by MC or MO

GS1 License Credentials in a Nutshell

A GS1 Prefix License Credential is issued by GO to a specific MO. It authorizes them to issue GCPs and GS1 ID Key Licenses from a specific GS1 Prefix value. GS1 Prefix License values are typically three digit numbers, but can range from two to seven digits with details in the [gs1-general-specification] Section 1.4.2. For example, GS1 New Zealand is issued prefixes 940 - 949.

A GCP License Credential is issued from an MO to a MC to authorize them to issue GS1 ID Keys from a specific number range. GCPs have different lengths depending on the needs of the MC. GCPs range from four digits to twelve digits. They always begin with (but are at least one digit longer than) the a valid GS1 Prefix License value. For details see the [gs1-general-specification] Section 1.4.4. The fewer digits a GCP has, the more individual GS1 ID Keys it can create. For example, a eleven digit GCP can only create a few GTINs while a 7 digit GCP can create many thousands of GTINs.

A GS1 ID Key License Credentials authorizes a MC to use a single GS1 ID Key for their business operations. GS1 ID Key Licenses are often used by small businesses to generate a few GTIN numbers for their products. They are also used to issue GLN numbers to identify organizations in the global supply chain that participate, but are not creating product, shipments or assets.

GS1 License Credentials Details

GS1 Licenses= Credentials use the W3C Verifiable Credentials Data Model Version 2.0 defined in [vc-data-model-2.0]. The VCDM credential format uses JSON-LD to encapsulate the details of a GS1 License in the format that is well understood worldwide. A few optional properties of the W3C VCDM are mandatory in GS1 License Credentials. These are outlined here:

License Credential Types

The GS1 License context establishes six different types of GS1 License Credentials. The following will be described in detail in this section:

  • GS1PrefixLicenseCredential -- A GS1 Prefix License
  • GS18PrefixLicenseCredential -- a GS1 8 Prefix License for issuing GTIN8 Keys
  • GS1CompanyPrefixLicenseCredential -- A GS1 Company Prefix License
  • GS1IdentificationKeyLicenseCredential -- A GS1 Single Issue ID Key License

This context also defines the following licenses that will be covered in section 3.5 License Credentials Exceptional Cases.

  • DelegatedGS1PrefixLicenseCredential
  • DelegatedGS1IdentificationKeyLicenseCredential

License Credential Name

License Credential name SHOULD convey a short name for the license types from Section 3.3.2 License Credential Types in in a human readable way for rendering in wallets. See 3.4 GS1 License Credentials Examples.

License Credential Description

License Credential description SHOULD convey a longer description of the license types from Section 3.3.2 License Credential Types in in a human readable way for rendering in wallets. See 3.4 GS1 License Credentials Examples.

licenseValue

A GS1 License Credentials MUST includes a licenseValue.

For GS1PrefixLicenseCredential, GS18PrefixLicenseCredential, GS1CompanyPrefixLicenseCredential, and DelegatedGS1PrefixLicenseCredential the licenseValue is the GS1 Prefix Or GCP as specified in [gs1-general-specification] Section 1.4.

For GS1IdentificationKeyLicenseCredential and DelegatedGS1IdentificationKeyLicenseCredential the licenseValue contains the licensed GS1 ID Key in the canonical form specified in [gs1-digital-link] Section 4.5.

alternativeLicenseValue

A GS1 License Credential MAY include an alternativeLicenseValue which bridges the canonical representations to compact representations for encoding into different symbologies. Typically this is the bridge between the U.P.C. or EAN system and the rest of the GS1 system.

GS1PrefixLicenseCredential, GS18PrefixLicenseCredential, GS1CompanyPrefixLicenseCredential, and DelegatedGS1PrefixLicenseCredential MAY contain an alternativeLicenseValue If the licenseValue starts with zero, then the alternativeLicenseValue is equilvalent to the licenseValue but without the leading zero.

For GS1IdentificationKeyLicenseCredential and DelegatedGS1IdentificationKeyLicenseCredential the alternativeLicenseValue MAY contain the compact form of the ID Key with leading zeros removed.

For example when licensing an individual GTIN in the United States an GS1IdentificationKeyLicenseCredential would contains a 14-digit licenseValue and a 12-digit alternativeLicenseValue, where the two values are different by two leading zeros.

Encapsulation and Signatures

GS1 License Credentials are issued in the W3C VCDM data model using [vc-jose-cose] specifically as referenced in Section 3.1. GS1 License Credentials from GO are signed with ECDSA ES256 algorithm. Contact your MO for details on signature algorithms. Examples are shown in Section 3.4 GS1 License Credentials Examples.

Delivery

GS18PrefixLicenseCredential and GS1PrefixLicenseCredential issued by GS1 Global Office are available in VC-JWT format via HTTP GET. The URL format is https://ref.gs1.org/vc/prefix/{XX} where {XX} is the license value.

Note: This is currently under development.

Contact your MO for details on obtaining GS1CompanyPrefixLicenseCredential and GS1IdentificationKeyLicenseCredential License Credentials.

VCDM Credential ID Property

The id property is optional in the VCDM, but MUST be present in GS1 License Credentials. This property SHOULD be chosen to be globally unique. Consideration should be given to revocation and re-issuance of credentials.

GS1 Global Office License credentials are identified with a credential ID property that is resolvable to the credential in VC-JWT format.

Note: This is currently under development.

VCDM Credential Subject ID property

The id property is optional in the VCDM, but MUST be present in GS1 License Credentials. The credential subject ID in all license credentials MUST be a DID that is controlled by the licensee. DIDs are defined by [did-1.1]

Validating GS1 License Credentials

GS1 License Credential validation is the process of confirming that a specific license is current, authentic, and relevant. Because GS1 Licenses are issued in a federated manner, license validation requires examining the set (chain) of licenses back to the GO root of trust.

Philosophy of License Validation

GS1 License Credential validation is the process by which a machine can verify that a GS1 License is authentic as part of the GS1 identity system. Generally, this involves validating the chain of GS1 License Credentials starting with the most specific license up to the root license issued by GS1 GO. In addition to checking the fields of the license documents, the documents must link together using the extendsCredential property forming a chain back to the root license. This chain must have properties so that each licenseValue stems from its previous licenseValue. This validation process is presented in detail in the sections below.

For example, in the chain with prefix license 022, GCP 02234545 and individual GTIN 02234545645654, we can see the licenseValue of the GTIN starts with the licenseValue of the GCP which starts with the licenseValue from the prefix license, indicating a complete and valid chain.

Validating a license chain ensures that assertions from the licensee are authentic and attributable to the party identified by the organization within the GS1 License Credential subject field. For specifics of GS1 identity validation policies consult your local MO.

License Validation Rules

The following section contains rules for validation of GS1 License Credentials. If any of the conditions referenced in the following lists are false, the credential is invalid and should not be trusted.

The following steps MUST be performed to validate a GS1 License Credential denoted as L:

  • L-1: L MUST be a valid VCDM 2.0 credential document.
  • L-2: The signature of L MUST be valid and MUST be from a signing key referenced in the issuer DID.
  • L-3: CredentialStatus of L MUST be valid (see 9. Validating Credential Status).
  • L-4: L validFrom MUST NOT be in the future.

Prefix License Validation Rules

The following additional steps MUST be performed to validate a GS1 Prefix License Credential denoted as PL:

  • PL-1: PL MUST conform to the JSON schema https://id.gs1.org/vc/schema/v1/prefix.
  • PL-2: PL Issuer property MUST be a GS1 GO did:web or did:webvh.
  • PL-3: If PL alternateLicenseValue is present, licenseValue must end with alternateLicenseValue.

GCP Validation Rules

The following additional steps MUST be performed to validate a GCP License Credential denoted as GL where the credential referenced by GL's extendsCredential property is denoted as EL:

  • GL-1: GL MUST conform to the JSON schema https://id.gs1.org/vc/schema/v1/companyprefix .
  • GL-2: EL MUST be a valid GS1 License Credential.
  • GL-3: EL credentialSubject idMUST match GL issuer.
  • GL-4: GL licenseValue MUST begin with and MUST be at least one digit longer than the EL licenseValue.
  • GL-5: If PL alternateLicenseValue is present, licenseValue must end with alternateLicenseValue.

Identification Key License Validation Rules

The following additional steps MUST be performed to validate a GS1 Identification Key License Credential denoted as IL where the credential referenced by IL's extendsCredential property is denoted as EL:

  • IL-1: IL MUST conform to the JSON schema https://id.gs1.org/vc/schema/v1/identificationkey.
  • IL-2: EL MUST be a valid GS1 License Credential.
  • IL-3: EL credentialSubject idMUST match IL issuer.
  • IL-4: IL licenseValue MUST begin with and MUST be at least one digit longer than the EL licenseValue
  • IL-5: If PL alternateLicenseValue is present, licenseValue must end with alternateLicenseValue.

Creating GS1 ID Key Credentials

This section describes how to create GS1 ID Key Credentials.

ID Key Overview

Section 2.2 GS1 Idenitfication Keys (GS1 ID Keys) described the various GS1 ID Key types that can be created from a GCP.

GS1 ID Key Credentials provide cryptographic proof that the GS1 identifier has been issued in full conformance with the GS1 identification system and its governance.

A MC that has obtained a GCP License Credential or GS1 ID Key License Credential can create GS1 ID Key Credentials to assert the existence of a specific GS1 ID Key. For example, if a fictitious company Healthy Tots has a GCP License Credential and wishes to commission a GTIN from their GCP, they can create and share a GS1 ID Key Credential asserting the existence of this GTIN for global trade.

If an MC uses serialization, batch/lot or other identifiers to further specify their individual trade items, the MC can also generate specific GS1 ID Key Credentials to assert the existence of a specific batch/lot or serial number of a trade item.

The specific format of each GS1 ID Key is different. This quick reference card highlights the basics of how to format GS1 ID Keys from a GCP. For more details on how to form individual GS1 ID Keys from your GCP, see your MO web page, the [gs1-general-specification] or services like GS1 Activate.

A GS1 ID Key License Credential still requires a GS1 ID Key Credential to assert that the GS1 ID Key is in circulation. Think of a GS1 ID Key License Credential as a right to use a specific GS1 ID Key and the GS1 ID Key Credential as the actual instantiation (usage) of that GS1 ID Key.

ID Key Credential Details

GS1 ID Key Credentials use the W3C Verifiable Credentials Data Model Version 2.0 defined in [vc-data-model-2.0]. The VCDM credential format uses JSON-LD to encapsulate the details in the format that is well understood worldwide.

A few optional properties of the W3C VCDM are mandatory in GS1 ID Key Credentials. These are outlined here:

Key Declaration Credential Context

The current GS1 Declaration Context can be found at https://ref.gs1.org/gs1/vc/declaration-context/.

ID Key Credential ID Property

The ID Key Credential ID Property SHOULD be a URI selected to be globally unique. If it is desired to have the credential publicly accessible the credential ID Property SHOULD be a resolvable URL.

ID Key CredentialSubject ID Property

The ID Key credentialSubject id property MUST be present and MUST be a GS1 Digital Link URI as defined in [gs1-digital-link].

ID Key Credential Name Property

The ID Key Credential name property MUST be present and SHOULD contain the following short string: "GS1 ID Key Credential"

ID Key Credential Description

The ID Key Credential description properly MUST be present and SHOULD contain the following description: "This is a Verifiable Credential that indicates that something has been identified. It contains no data about what has been identified as that is done via the association process. This credential is used only to indicate that the GS1 ID Key that it contains exists and is valid."

ID Key Credential extendsCredential property

If the credentialSubject of the GS1 ID Key credential contains a GS1 Digital Link with a primary key K and no key qualifiers, then the extendsCredential property MUST contain a link to a GS1 License Credenial whose licenseValue is the string that starts K.

If the credentialSubject of the GS1 ID Key credential contains a GS1 Digital Link with a primary key K and key qualifiers Q then the extendsCredential property MUST contain a link to a GS1 ID Key Credential whose credentialSubject contains a GS1 Digital Link with the same primary key K and no key qualifiers.

For a description of primary keys and key qualifiers see [gs1-digital-link].

A few examples:

  • A GS1 ID Key credential with credentialSubject id https://id.gs1.org/01/07541234555551 properly extends a GCP credential with licenseValue 0754123
  • A GS1 ID Key credential with credentialSubject id https://id.gs1.org/01/07541234555551 does NOT properly extend a GCP credential with licenseValue 0755555
  • A GS1 ID Key credential with credentialSubject id https://id.gs1.org/01/07541234555551/10/ABC123 properly extends a GS1 ID Key Credential with credentialSubject id https://id.gs1.org/01/07541234555551
  • A GS1 ID Key credential with credentialSubject id https://id.gs1.org/01/07541234555551/10/ABC123 does NOT properly extend a GS1 ID Key Credential with credentialSubject id https://id.gs1.org/01/07541234777777
  • A GS1 ID Key credential with credentialSubject id https://id.gs1.org/01/07541234555551/10/ABC123 does NOT properly extend a GS1 ID Key Credential with credentialSubject id https://id.gs1.org/01/07541234555551/10/ABC123 because although it has the same primary key, it contains a key qualifier.

Validating GS1 ID Key Credential

This section describes the philosophy and process for validating GS1 ID Key Credentials.

GS1 ID Key Validation Overview

A GS1 ID Key Credential provides proof that a GS1 MC has commissioned a specific GS1 ID Key, possibly with additional specificity like a specific batch/lot or specific serial number. The validation of a GS1 ID Key Credential requires verifying the integrity of the credential itself as well as validating that the Key Credential "extends" from a valid Credential. Validating a GS1 ID Key Credential provide cryptographic proof that the GS1 identifier has been issued in full conformance with the GS1 identification system and its governance.

GS1 ID Key Validation Rules

Denoting the GS1 ID Key Credential as K, K's credentialSubject id as D, and the credential referenced by K's extendsCredential property as P, the following steps MUST be performed to validate a GS1 ID Key Credential:
  • K-1: K MUST be a valid VCDM 2.0 credential document.
  • K-2: The signature of K MUST be valid and from a signing key referenced in the issuer DID.
  • K-3: CredentialStatus of L MUST be valid (see 9. Validating Credential Status).
  • K-4: K validFrom MUST NOT be in the future.
  • K-5: The credential document K MUST conform to the JSON schema https://id.gs1.org/vc/schema/v1/key.
  • K-6: D must be a GS1 Digital Link as defined in [gs1-digital-link]
  • K-7: If D contains a GS1 Digital Link with primary Key PK and no key qualfiers:
    • K-7.1: P MUST be a valid GS1 License Credential.
    • K-7.2: The issuer of K MUST match the subject of P.
    • K-7.3: PK MUST begin with (as a string) the licenseValue from P.
  • K-8: If D contains a GS1 Digital Link with primary Key PK and one or more key qualifiers:
    • K-8.1: The issuer of K MUST match the issuer of P.
    • K-8.2: P MUST be a valid GS1 ID Key Credential.
    • K-8.3: The primary key of P's credentialSubject id MUST equal PK.

Key Validation Libraries and Tools

GS1 has developed tools for key and license validation hosted at https://github.com/gs1/vc-data-model-verifier

Creating GS1 Data Credentials

This section describes how to create GS1 Data Credentials.

What is a GS1 Data Credential

A GS1 Data Credential is an envelope to declare facts about GS1 ID Keys. Its primary purpose is to link back to the Key Credentials (and ultimately the License) so that these facts can be confidently attributed to the MC that holds the license to commission this GS1 ID Key. For example, as a brand owner, I can create a GS1 Data Credential that asserts the country of origin of a specific GTIN/Batch/Lot and any party receiving this credential (and its chain) can know its authentic data from the brand owner.

GS1 Data Credential Details

GS1 Data Credentials use the W3C Verifiable Credentials Data Model Version 2.0 defined in [vc-data-model-2.0]. The VCDM credential format uses JSON-LD to encapsulate the details in the format that is well understood worldwide. A few optional properties of the W3C VCDM are mandatory in GS1 Data Credentials. These are outlined here:

GS1 Data Credential VCDM core properties
Property Notes
@context In addition to the W3C VCDM 2.0 Context, the GS1 Declaration Credential Context MUST be included. See Section 7.2.1 Data Declaration Credential Context.
id This property is optional in the VCDM but MUST be present in all GS1 Data Credentials. See Section 7.2.2 Data Credential IDs.
type This property MUST contain VerifiableCredential and MUST also contain DataCredential.
issuer:id This property MUST be a Decentralized Identifier (DID) as defined in [did-1.1].
credentialSubject:id This property is optional in the VCDM but MUST be present and MUST be a GS1 Digital Link URI as defined in [gs1-digital-link]. See Section 5.2.3 ID Key CredentialSubject ID Property.
credentialStatus This property is optional in the VCDM but MAY be present. If present, this property MUST be of the type BitstringStatusListEntry defined in [vc-bitstring-status-list].
credentialSchema This property is optional in the VCDM but MUST be present in all GS1 Data Credentials and MUST be of the type JsonSchema defined in [vc-json-schema].
validFrom This property is optional in the VCDM but MUST be present in all GS1 Data Credentials.
renderMethod This property is optional in the VCDM but SHOULD be present and SHOULD be of the type TemplateRenderMethod in [vc-render-method].

All GS1 Data Credentials share these additional properties:

GS1 Data Credential Properties
Property Type Presence Description
keyAuthorization xsd:anyURI Optional a reference to a credential that authorizes the issuer to declare data. Normally this is a GS1 ID Key Credential. When the Licensee of the GS1 ID Key creates Data Credentials they SHOULD include a keyAuthorization referencing the GS1 ID Key Credential for this ID Key.

Data Declaration Credential Context

The current GS1 Declaration Context can be found at https://ref.gs1.org/gs1/vc/declaration-context/.

Data Credential IDs

The Data Credential id property SHOULD be a URI selected to be globally unique. If its desired to have the credential publicly accessible the credential ID Property SHOULD be a resolvable URL.

Data CredentialSubject ID Property

The Data credentialSubject id property MUST be present and MUST be a GS1 Digital Link URI defined in [gs1-digital-link].

Data Credential Subtypes

GS1 Data Credentials form a base class for building more specific credentials. GS1 defines these specific Data Credentials that extend the DataCredential model for various GS1 ID Keys.

Product Data Credential

A GS1 Product Data Credential is a DataCredential with the following properties

GS1 Product Data Credential Properties
Property Type Presence Description
product gs1:Product Mandatory An Object that contains an instance of gs1:Product describing the product associated with credentialSubject id GTIN GS1 Digital Link URI.
In addition:
  • The @context property must also contain https://ref.gs1.org/gs1/vc/product-context

Organization Data Credential

A GS1 Organization Data Credential is a DataCredential with the following properties

GS1 Organization Data Credential Properties
Property Type Presence Description
organization gs1:Organization Mandatory An Object that contains an instance of gs1:Organization describing the organization associated with credentialSubject id GLN GS1 Digital Link URI.
In addition:
  • The @context property must also contain https://ref.gs1.org/gs1/vc/organization-context

Extending a Data Credential

GS1 Data Credentials are meant to be extended to allow MCs to assert any facts about any GS1 ID Keys

Custom Extensions to the GS1 Data Credential should be formed as follows:

  • The type property MUST contain DataCredential and satisfy the requirements in 7.2 GS1 Data Credential Details.
  • The type property SHOULD contain a credential-specific type (e.g., CertificationDetailsCredential).
  • The credential SHOULD contain a schema reference to your custom credential.
  • The data should describe the GS1 ID Key that is the credentialSubject id.
  • When possible the data SHOULD adhere to the GS1 Web Vocabulary or the GS1 Global Data Model.

Validating GS1 Data Credentials

This section describes the philosophy and procedures to validate GS1 Data Credentials.

What is Data Validation

GS1 Data Credential validation is the process of validating a set of data about a GS1 ID Key.

For example, a GS1 Data Credential about a GS1 Digital Link describing a GTIN might assert master data about the product such as dimensions, color, sizing, description, country of origin or any other attribute. In this case, it's desirable to verify that this data has not been altered and was created by the licensee of that specific GTIN.

Another data credential might offer details about a specific shipment (SSCC), including the date, time, and location the shipment was received by a specific party. In this case, it's important to verify that this data was unaltered, but it is unlikely this would be issued by the licensee of the SSCC.

Data Validation Overview

Validating a data credential without a keyAuthorization property is simply ensuring that the credential is well-formed and has not been altered. When a data credential contains a keyAuthorization, its best practice to validate the chain of credentials linked via this authorization. Data Validation Rules

Denoting the GS1 ID Data Credential as D, the following steps MUST be performed to validate a GS1 Data Credential
  • D-1: D MUST be a valid VCDM 2.0 credential document.
  • D-2: The signature of D MUST be valid and from a signing key referenced in the issuer DID.
  • D-3: If present, CredentialStatus of D MUST be valid (see 9. Validating Credential Status).
  • D-4: D validFrom MUST NOT be in the future.
  • D-5: The credential document D MUST conform to the JSON schema for the data credential:
    • D-5.1: For general data credentials https://id.gs1.org/vc/schema/v1/data
    • D-5.2: For product data credentials https://id.gs1.org/vc/schema/v1/productdata
    • D-5.3: For organization data credentials https://id.gs1.org/vc/schema/v1/organizationdata
    • D-5.4: For custom data credentials, any additional schemas defined within the credential
If the credential D contains a keyAuthorization property, denoting the credential referenced by this property as K, it's possible to verify that the data in this credential was created by the licensee of the GS1 ID Key. The following steps SHOULD be performed:
  • D-1: The issuer of D MUST match the issuer of K
  • D-2: K MUST be a valid GS1 ID Key Credential
  • D-3: The canonicalized D credentialSubject id MUST match the canonicalized K credentialSubject id

Tricky Topics

This section captures some exceptional cases within the GS1 system and provides guidance on how to handle these cases when encountered in GS1 Digital Licenses. It's recommended that users interested in these topics review the GS1 System Architecture.

GS1 ID Key Tiers

Within the GS1 identification system, keys are broken down into four tiers. Full details may be found in the GS1 System Architecture; some of the text is reproduced below.

  • Tier 1: The structure, usage, and lifecycle rules of a GS1 tier 1 key are defined, administered, and managed entirely by GS1. A tier 1 key always incorporates a GS1 Prefix. A tier 1 key may incorporate a GS1 Company Prefix issued to a user company, who then issues the key, or it may be issued in its entirety as an individual key. Tier 1 keys are subject to allocation rules defined in GS1 standards, and their association with descriptive data elements is governed by validation rules also defined in GS1 standards.
  • Tier 2: The structure of a GS1 tier 2 key is defined by GS1 as a GS1 tier 1 key, but its usage and lifecycle rules are defined, administered, and managed by an external organization. A tier 2 key exists within the range of a GS1 Prefix or a GS1 Company Prefix, incorporates additional characters administered by an external organization, and includes a check digit or a check character pair if required by its corresponding tier 1 key format. Tier 2 keys are unique with respect to tier 1 keys of the same type and can be used in most or all applications that support the corresponding tier 1 key type. Their allocation and lifecycle rules, however, are defined by an organization external to GS1. The degree to which the usage and lifecycle rules are compatible with those of the corresponding tier 1 keys is specific to each tier 2 key.
  • Tier 3: The structure, usage, and lifecycle rules of a GS1 tier 3 key are defined, administered, and managed entirely by an organization external to GS1. This organization enters into an agreement with GS1 that enables its keys to be supported in selected GS1 standards (e.g., within an EPC header).
  • Tier 4: The structure, usage, and lifecycle rules of a GS1 tier 4 key are defined, administered, and managed entirely by an organization or entity external to GS1. A tier 4 key has no explicit support within the GS1 system, but it may have some implicit support. For example, the EPCIS standard supports any URI as an object identifier and trading partners could, by mutual agreement, agree to use URIs for geographic locations as a ReadPointID for certain events.

Note: The GS1 Digital License system currently handles Tier 1 ID Keys with limited support for Tier 2 ID Keys.

Delegated GS1 ID Key Issuance

Tier 2 GS1 ID Keys are delegated to organizations outside GS1. For example, the International Standard Book Number (ISBN), issued by the International ISBN Agency. GS1 Prefixes 978 and 979 are delegated to the Agency for ISBN management.

Currently, GS1 has not issued credentials to Tier 2 Key Organizations, so it's unlikely that these will be encountered. However, the Digital License system has provisions to support GS1 Prefix Licenses issued to Tier 2 Key management organizations like ISBN. These credentials will appear as GS1DelegatedPrefixLicenseCredential. They can be treated like a GS1PrefixLicenseCredential with the following additions:

  • GS1DelegatedPrefixLicenseCredential can only extend GS1IdentificationKeyLicenseCredential
  • When validating an GS1IdentificationKeyLicenseCredential that extends a GS1DelegatedPrefixLicenseCredential, the identificationKeyType property of the GS1IdentificationKeyLicenseCredential must be supported within the identificationKeyType property of the GS1DelegatedPrefixLicenseCredential

Note: Support for validation of ID Keys with delegated Prefix Issuance may not be well supported in available tools.
